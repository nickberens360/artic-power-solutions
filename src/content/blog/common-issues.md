When an Appleton RollMover™ roll pusher or CartMover™ cart tugger starts acting up, the whole line feels it. The good news is that most problems trace back to a short list of causes, and the most common one is also the easiest to fix: a worn-out battery.

Work through these five problems in order. Start with the battery before assuming something more expensive has failed.

***Safety first:*** *Power the unit off before inspecting it, and follow your owner's manual. If you aren't comfortable doing a check, or the problem continues, take the unit out of service and have it looked at by a qualified technician.*

## 1. The Battery Dies Quickly or Won't Charge

This is the problem we hear about most, and it's usually the root of several others on this list.

**Symptoms:**
- Runtime has dropped to a fraction of a shift.
- The charger shows a full charge, but the mover dies soon after.
- The pack won't take a charge at all, or won't hold one overnight.

**What to check:**
- **The charger connection.** Make sure the charger is firmly plugged into both the outlet and the unit. A loose plug is an easy thing to miss.
- **The charger's indicator lights.** If your charger has status lights, check its manual to see whether they're signaling a fault.
- **The battery terminals and connector.** Look for corrosion, loose connections or damaged wiring. A poor connection can look exactly like a bad battery.
- **Runtime after a full charge.** Charge the pack fully, then note how long the mover runs under normal work. If it's well short of what it used to be, the cells are likely worn out.
- **The battery's age.** Rechargeable packs wear out with use. If yours is several years old and runtime has been steadily dropping, it's time for a replacement.

## 2. It Slows Down or Stalls Under a Full Load

A mover that handles light loads fine but bogs down with a full roll or a heavy cart is often showing the next stage of a failing battery. Worn cells can't hold their voltage under heavy demand, so the unit loses power exactly when it needs it most.

**What to check:**
- **Battery condition.** If runtime is also dropping (see #1), the battery is the most likely cause.
- **The load.** Make sure you're within the unit's rated capacity for the floor surface. Ramps and transitions reduce how much a mover can push.
- **Drag on the wheels.** Look for shrink wrap, strapping or debris wrapped around the wheels or rollers.

## 3. It Won't Move at All

You press the throttle and nothing happens.

**What to check:**
- **Battery charge.** A fully depleted pack is the most common cause. Charge it and try again.
- **Safety and stop controls.** Many walk-behind units have an emergency stop or reverse control on the handle. Make sure nothing has been tripped or is stuck.
- **The battery connector.** Confirm the pack is fully seated and the connector is locked in place.
- **Obstructions.** Check that nothing is jammed around the wheels.

If the battery is charged, the controls are clear and it still won't move, the problem is likely electrical or in the drive system. That's a job for a qualified technician.

## 4. Unusual Noises During Operation

Grinding, squealing or clicking that wasn't there before is a sign something needs attention.

**What to check:**
- **Loose hardware.** Look for loose bolts, panels or guards.
- **Debris.** Check the wheels and rollers for anything caught in them.
- **Your manual.** Follow the manufacturer's guidance on lubrication points and service intervals.

Noise that continues after a basic inspection usually means wear in the drive components. Have it checked before it causes more damage.

## 5. The Motor Is Running Hot

An overheating motor should be addressed right away.

**Symptoms:**
- The motor housing is too hot to touch comfortably.
- You notice a burning smell.

**What to check:**
- **Overloading.** Pushing loads beyond the unit's rating makes the motor work harder and run hotter.
- **Blockages.** Anything dragging on the wheels forces the motor to work harder.
- **Dust buildup.** In paper and converting plants especially, dust on the motor traps heat. Keep it clean. [Here's how print and paper plants can reduce downtime](/blog/equipment-downtime).

If you notice a burning smell, stop using the unit and have it inspected.
