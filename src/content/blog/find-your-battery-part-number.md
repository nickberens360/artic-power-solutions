Ordering a replacement battery for an Appleton RollMover™ or CartMover™ is simple once you know one thing: the **Appleton part number** of the pack you're replacing. Match that number and you know the voltage, size and connector are right.

Here's where to find it, what the common numbers mean, and what to do if the label is missing or worn.

## Where to Look

The part number is printed on the label of the **battery pack itself**, not on the mover's frame. To find it:

1. **Power the unit off** and disconnect it from the charger.
2. **Locate the battery pack.** Follow your owner's manual if you need to open a cover or remove the pack to see its label.
3. **Read the label.** Look for a six-digit Appleton part number, such as 139082, 137819 or 143242.
4. **Take a photo.** A clear photo of the label is handy for ordering and for your maintenance records.

## What the Common Part Numbers Mean

| Appleton part # | Equipment | Voltage | Replacement options |
|---|---|---|---|
| [139082](/parts/appleton-139082-battery) | RollMover™ roll pusher | 36V | 3000mAh NiCd or 5000mAh NiMH |
| [137819](/parts/appleton-137819-battery) | CartMover™ cart tugger | 24V | 3000mAh NiCd |
| [143242](/parts/appleton-143242-battery) | CartMover™ cart tugger | 24V | 9000mAh NiMH |

A few things to note:

- **The two CartMover™ numbers are different packs.** Both are 24V, but #137819 and #143242 aren't interchangeable just because the voltage matches. Order the number on your label. See our [CartMover™ battery comparison](/blog/cartmover-battery-137819-vs-143242) for details.
- **For #139082 you can choose a chemistry.** NiCd is the like-for-like option and handles cold well. NiMH stores about 67% more energy at the same voltage, for longer runtime per charge.

## If the Label Is Missing or Unreadable

Labels get scuffed, faded or torn off over years of use. If you can't read yours, gather what you can:

- **The equipment model.** Is it a RollMover™ roll pusher or a CartMover™ cart tugger? Note any model name or serial plate on the unit.
- **The voltage.** Your charger's label usually lists its output voltage, which tells you whether you need a 24V or 36V pack.
- **Photos.** Take pictures of the battery pack, its connector and the battery compartment.

Then [send us what you have](#contact). We'll help you identify the right pack before you order.

## Keep the Number on File

Once you know your part number, write it down in your maintenance log or on the unit's service tag. Next time a pack wears out, ordering takes a minute instead of a teardown.
