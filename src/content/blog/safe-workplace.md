An Appleton RollMover™ makes moving heavy rolls faster and far easier on your team. Like any powered material handling equipment, it needs to be operated with care to prevent accidents and injuries.

These seven best practices help keep operators and everyone around them safe. Always follow your owner's manual and your facility's safety procedures first.

## 1. Do a Pre-Shift Inspection

Before each shift, the operator should do a quick walk-around.

- Check the frame, handle and wheels for visible damage.
- Make sure the battery has enough charge for the work ahead.
- Test the emergency stop or reverse control and any horn or alarm.
- Confirm the throttle and controls respond correctly.

A machine in good working order is a safer machine. Our [maintenance checklist](/blog/maintenance-checklist) covers daily, weekly and monthly checks.

## 2. Stay Aware of Your Surroundings

- Scan your path for people, obstacles and other equipment.
- Slow down at blind corners, doorways and intersections.
- Keep a safe distance from racks, walls and other rolls.

## 3. Use a Proper Stance and Grip

- Walk behind the unit, not beside it, unless your manual says otherwise.
- Keep a firm grip on the handle.
- Move at a controlled walking pace. Never run.

## 4. Handle the Load Carefully

- Make sure the mover is properly engaged with the roll before moving it.
- Start and stop smoothly so the load doesn't shift unexpectedly.
- Never exceed the unit's rated capacity, and remember that ramps and transitions reduce it.

## 5. Charge and Handle Batteries Safely

Battery packs are heavy and store a lot of energy.

- Charge in a designated, well-ventilated area away from heat sources.
- Inspect charging cables and plugs for damage before each use.
- Use only a charger that matches the pack's voltage and chemistry.
- Lift packs with proper technique, or get help.
- Keep tools and metal objects away from battery terminals.

## 6. Park and Store the Unit Properly

- Park in a designated area away from walkways and high-traffic zones.
- Turn the unit off when it isn't in use.
- Connect it to the charger at the end of the shift so it's ready for the next one.

## 7. Take Malfunctioning Units Out of Service

Never operate a mover you suspect isn't working properly.

- If something seems wrong during inspection or use, stop and tag the unit out of service.
- Report unusual noises, overheating, burning smells or control problems to your supervisor.
- Have mechanical and electrical problems repaired by a qualified technician.

A unit that slows down or stalls under load often has a worn-out battery. Our [troubleshooting guide](/blog/common-issues) explains how to tell.

## Safety Is a Team Effort

A safe workplace takes ongoing effort from everyone. Build these habits into your daily routine, and your roll movers will stay a productive, safe tool for your operation.
