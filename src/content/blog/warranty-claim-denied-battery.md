You file a warranty claim for a weak battery in your Appleton RollMover™ or CartMover™, and it comes back denied. It's frustrating, but it's also common, because rechargeable batteries wear out with normal use.

Here's why it happens and what your options are.

## Why Battery Claims Get Denied

Equipment warranties generally cover defects in materials and workmanship, not parts that wear out through normal use. Batteries lose capacity with every charge cycle, so a pack that has simply reached the end of its life is often treated as **normal wear and tear**.

Other common reasons include a warranty period that has expired, or damage from charging, storage or handling that falls outside the manufacturer's guidelines.

*Every warranty is different. Read your own terms, and ask the manufacturer or dealer if anything is unclear.*

## Step 1: Understand the Decision

Before deciding what to do next, get clear on the details:

- **Ask for the reason in writing.** Knowing exactly why the claim was denied tells you whether it's worth pursuing.
- **Check the battery's coverage.** Some warranties cover batteries for a different period than the rest of the unit.
- **Gather your records.** Purchase date, service history and charging practices all matter if you decide to appeal.

## Step 2: Confirm It's Really the Battery

A denied claim doesn't change the fact that your mover needs to work. Make sure the battery is the problem before you replace anything:

- Runtime has dropped well below a full shift.
- The unit slows or stalls under load even after a full charge.
- The charger shows full, but the pack dies quickly.

Rule out loose connectors and charger faults first. Our [troubleshooting guide](/blog/common-issues) covers the checks.

## Step 3: Choose a Replacement

If the battery is worn out and not covered, you have two paths:

- **An original (OEM) replacement** from the manufacturer or a dealer.
- **A compatible replacement** matched to the Appleton part number on your current pack.

Compatible packs are usually less expensive and often ship faster. For the 36V RollMover™ (#139082), you can also choose NiMH for longer runtime per charge. See [OEM vs. compatible RollMover™ batteries](/blog/oem-vs-compatible) for a full comparison.

## If It's Not the Battery

If your checks point to the motor, drive or electrical system instead, contact Appleton Mfg. or a qualified material-handling service shop for a repair quote.

## Get Back to Work

A denied claim is a setback, not a dead end. If a worn-out battery is the problem, a replacement pack matched to your part number will get the mover running again. [Contact us](#contact) if you need help identifying the right one.
