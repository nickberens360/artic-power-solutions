Every rechargeable battery wears out eventually, including the pack in your Appleton RollMover™ or CartMover™. How long it lasts depends less on the calendar and more on how hard it's used and how it's treated.

Here's how to estimate battery life, what shortens it, and how to tell when a pack is ready to be replaced.

## Battery Life Is Measured in Charge Cycles

Battery life is usually rated in **charge cycles**: one full discharge and recharge. As a pack goes through cycles, its capacity slowly drops until it can no longer get through a shift.

## A Rough Way to Estimate Years of Service

To turn cycles into time, estimate how many full charges your unit goes through in a year.

For example, a mover charged once per workday goes through roughly 250 cycles a year. At that pace, a pack rated for 1,000 cycles works out to about four years. A unit charged twice a day in a two-shift operation would use those cycles in about half the time.

Treat this as a ballpark, not a guarantee. Real-world life depends on the factors below.

## What Shortens Battery Life

- **Heat.** High temperatures are hard on batteries. Charging or storing packs near heaters, ovens or in direct sun shortens their life.
- **Extreme cold.** Cold reduces available runtime while it lasts. NiCd handles cold better than NiMH. Our NiCd packs are rated to operate down to -40°C, and our 24V NiMH pack down to -20°C.
- **Heavy loads.** Pushing loads near the unit's rated capacity, or up ramps, drains the pack faster and means more cycles per shift.
- **Charging habits.** Using the wrong charger, or not following the charger's instructions, can damage cells.
- **Long idle periods.** A pack left discharged for weeks or months can lose capacity. Store packs charged and top them up periodically.

## Signs Your Battery Is Wearing Out

- **Runtime has dropped.** The mover used to last a full shift and now needs a mid-shift charge.
- **It struggles under load.** The unit slows down or stalls with a full roll or a heavy cart, even after a full charge.
- **The charger says full, but it dies fast.** The pack takes a charge but can't hold its voltage under work.
- **It won't hold a charge overnight.** A fully charged pack is noticeably weaker the next morning.

If you're seeing these, rule out the simple things first, like loose connectors or a charger fault. Our [troubleshooting guide](/blog/common-issues) walks through the checks.

## Getting the Most From Every Pack

- Charge with the correct charger in a cool, ventilated area.
- Keep packs away from heat sources.
- Track runtime monthly so you can see decline coming.
- Order a replacement before the pack fails, so a worn battery never becomes unplanned downtime.
